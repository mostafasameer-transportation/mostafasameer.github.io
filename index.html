My journey into engineering began with the 2013 Rana Plaza collapse in Dhaka — a tragedy that exposed the profound responsibility engineers carry. Watching 1,134 lives lost to structural failure planted a seed that grew into a career devoted to building safer, more resilient environments.

Today I work under Dr. Wallied Orabi at Florida International University, focusing on hurricane-resistant infrastructure and flood risk assessment. My research uses machine learning, GIS, and computational modeling to understand and reduce the vulnerability of coastal communities.

Before FIU, I worked as a quality technician on the $5.6B Gordie Howe International Bridge, ensuring AASHTO and ASTM compliance. I also interned at Fluor Corporation on large-scale infrastructure projects.

"Every structure I help design carries the weight of countless lives depending on it to stand — not just today, but for generations."
